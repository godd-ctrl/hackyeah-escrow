The problem

Two people who have never met
need a third one to hold the money.

And that third party is not a neutral pipe. It is a business with its own incentives, and every property that makes it useful is a property it can also abuse.

01

Custody

The platform holds the balance. Your money is a row in its database, withdrawable when it decides — and frozen when it decides otherwise.

02

Discretion

Disputes are settled by an employee reading messages, with no rule published in advance and no appeal that is not the same employee again.

03

Permission

Being paid requires an account, a country, and a reputation score. The work can be finished and still not be payable.

The fee is the visible cost. The invisible one is that neither side can verify the other's money exists before the work starts — which is exactly why the platform gets to charge it.

The mechanism

Four instructions replace the middleman.

No custody, no dispute desk, no account. The rules are the program, and the program is 126 bytes of readable state.

CREATE

Client locks the whole job

The amount moves from the client's token account into a vault PDA. The escrow account is derived from [client, mint, deadline], so the address is predictable before it exists.

→
ACCEPT

Freelancer sees the money

Accepting binds their key to the escrow. They are now working against a balance they can read on-chain, not a promise.

→
APPROVE

Each milestone releases

One approval moves amount / milestones. Neither side can take the whole pot early, and a remainder is rejected at creation so nothing can be stranded.

→
CLAIM

Silence has a terminus

If the client never reviews, the freelancer claims the balance after the deadline. The platform's only irreplaceable job — deciding what happens when someone goes quiet — is now a timestamp.

Why this is not just "escrow on a chain". The deadline branch is what makes it a substitute rather than a tool. Without it, a client who stops answering keeps the money forever and you still need a platform to complain to.

03 / 10
What the program enforces

Every promise is a constraint,
not a policy.

PropertyHow it is enforced
The client cannot take it back After acceptance the vault is owned by the escrow PDA. cancel requires status == Open, so once work has started the refund path is closed to the client.
The freelancer cannot take it early claim_unreviewed requires the on-chain clock to have passed deadline. The clock is the chain's, not the server's.
Only the right person acts Each instruction declares its signer; has_one binds the escrow to the client, and the payout address is checked against escrow.mint.
No funds are stranded Creation rejects an amount that does not divide evenly by the milestone count, so the vault can always be emptied by approvals or by a claim.
Two jobs cannot collide The PDA includes the client, the mint and the deadline, so a second job with identical terms cannot silently overwrite the first.

Deliberately absent: an admin key, an upgrade-authority escape hatch in the instruction set, and any instruction that can move the vault without either a milestone approval or a passed deadline.

The product

It is a page you can open,
not a diagram of one.

One file. No build step, no bundler, no npm install. Connect a browser wallet, mint demo money, post a job, accept it from a second wallet, release it milestone by milestone, and watch the escrow state change on the explorer.

  • Decoded, not raw. The panel renders the account — client, freelancer, mint, released milestones, vault balance, deadline — instead of an address list.
  • Role-aware buttons. A button that would fail is disabled; the program is still the authority, this is only courtesy.
  • Honest when it cannot work. If the program is not at the configured address the page says so in a banner, rather than letting six buttons fail one at a time.

Who it is for

The first job between strangers.

Not the agency with a procurement department. The person taking a first job from someone in another country, where the only thing either side knows about the other is that they are on the internet.

That is the transaction where trust has to be manufactured, and it is the one platforms charge the most for — because they are selling the existence of the money, not the transfer of it.

THE ASYMMETRY WE REMOVE

The freelancer starts work against a promise. The client pays against a hope. Both are buying the same thing: proof the other side will not vanish.

A vault anyone can read, with a rule that fires without asking, is that proof — and it does not need either party to be trusted, or even to have a bank account.

Distribution

Ships as a link. No install, no account, no KYC — the wallet is the identity, which is also the onboarding cost.

Extensibility

A second party (an arbiter) and a dispute branch are additive instructions, not a rewrite. The custody core stays untouched.

Cost

Two transaction fees and rent that is refunded on close. The platform's percentage is the thing being deleted.
